About Greenwood Nature Sanctuary

Founded in 1948, Greenwood Nature Sanctuary is dedicated to preserving indigenous woodland ecosystems, supporting biodiversity, and reconnecting communities with the natural world.

Our Sustainable Forest Canopy Trail

To allow guests to observe fragile forest floor ecosystems without causing soil compaction or root disruption, our team engineered the elevated Rainforest Canopy Trail. This two-mile wooden boardwalk meanders gently through old-growth cedar, oak, and mossy fern groves.

Our Conservation History

Originally deeded by botanist Dr. Eleanor Greenwood in the late 1940s, the land was protected from commercial timber harvesting and residential subdivisions. What began as a 50-acre private botanical collection has expanded into a premier 300-acre biological preserve.

Through seven decades of continuous stewardship, our botanists have documented over 650 native plant species, 140 resident and migratory bird species, and dozens of mammals, amphibians, and beneficial insect pollinators that thrive in our continuous riparian corridors.

Today, our non-profit trust operates an active seed bank, a wildlife rehabilitation center, and outdoor classrooms that welcome more than 25,000 schoolchildren every year for immersive environmental science workshops.

1. Ecological Habitat Protection

We safeguard contiguous wildlife corridors, preventing habitat fragmentation and providing safe sanctuary for migratory species along the Pacific Flyway.

2. Scientific Research & Seed Banking

Our botanical laboratory preserves cryo-stored seeds of endangered regional flora, ensuring genetic resilience against climate pressures.

3. Public Educational Outreach

We offer guided citizen-science field days, public workshops on drought-tolerant gardening, and hands-on nature discovery programs for youth.
